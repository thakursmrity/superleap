Prospect agent: new pipeline, from what your CRM already knows

A rep today is three people: a researcher finding accounts, an SDR writing to them, and the judge of what is safe to send. The agent does the first two from the team's own closed-won history. The rep keeps the judgement, and hands it over only as the agent earns it.

Product vision

1.Problem

Superleap's agents work the leads a team already has. New pipeline still comes from separate tools that sit outside the CRM, guess at fit from generic filters, and push the same sequence to everyone. Reps spend hours researching before they write a word. Then teams either review every message, losing the time AI should save, or send everything and burn their domain and their brand.

2.Users and jobs

UserJob to be doneWhat they get
Rep or SDR (primary)Spend time in conversations, not researchA ready queue of accounts with a reason to reach out, a draft, and a clear call on what needs them
Sales headBuild outbound pipeline without a separate tool stackPipeline sourced inside the CRM, with fit learned from their own wins
RevOps or adminKeep data clean and the brand safeSuppression, claim checks and sending limits enforced before anything goes out
Compliance (BFSI customers)Stop regulated claims reaching prospectsA hard flag on any claim outside the approved library, whatever the confidence

3.How it works

  1. Learn who buysBuild the profile from the team's own closed-won deals: industry, size, channels, timing, first contact.
  2. Watch for why nowFunding, hiring, expansion, a champion changing jobs, or engagement like pricing page visits.
  3. Find the personMap the buying committee, verify the contact, and suppress anyone already in a deal or opted out.
  4. Write from the signalDraft in the team's voice, opening with the reason for writing, using only approved claims and proof.
  5. Route and learnScore confidence and stakes, send to a lane, and learn from every approve, edit and reject.

4.Confidence-Tiered Autonomy

Two separate scores decide each message. Confidence asks "is this right?" Stakes ask "how bad is it if it's wrong?" Flags override both.

Sends on its own

  • Confidence above this rep's earned bar
  • Low stakes: a follow-up, a reply, a short intro
  • Verified contact, no flags

Waits for the rep

  • Medium confidence, such as an inferred job title
  • High stakes: a named target account, a lost deal, a senior first touch
  • The rep edits or approves in one tap

Flagged

  • Contact in someone else's open deal
  • Job change, bounced email or opt-out
  • A claim outside the approved library, or a regulated claim

The bar moves per rep and per message type. Approvals without edits lower it slowly. Edits and rejects raise it quickly. Trust is slow to earn and fast to lose.

5.Prospecting principles built in

1

Signal before sequence

No reason to write, no message. Every draft opens with why now, not with the product.

2

Suppress before you send

Customers, open deals, opt-outs and past bounces are removed before a draft is ever written.

3

Fewer, better messages

Daily caps per sending inbox and per account. Two people at one company never get the same email on the same day.

4

Protect the domain

Verify every email, watch bounces and spam complaints, and pause sending the moment they climb.

5

Opt-in before WhatsApp

Cold first touch stays on email. WhatsApp is used only where the person has opted in.

6

Every claim has a source

Numbers, case studies and pricing come only from an approved library. Anything else is flagged.

7.First version

In scope

  • Profile learned from the customer's own closed-won deals
  • Four signals: funding, hiring, job change, pricing or webinar engagement
  • Email drafts in the team's voice, from an approved claims library
  • Suppression against customers, open deals and opt-outs
  • Three lanes, with the bar earned per rep
  • Sending caps and bounce monitoring per inbox

Out of scope for v1

  • Cold WhatsApp or SMS first touches
  • Auto-send to named target accounts
  • Multi-step sequences that send without the rep
  • Phone and voice outreach
  • Buying contact data on the customer's behalf

8.Risks and mitigations

RiskLikelihoodMitigation
An auto-sent message embarrasses the brandMediumOnly low-stakes types can ever auto-send; trust drops fast after a reject; one switch pauses autonomy for the team
Sending domain gets flagged as spamMediumVerified emails only, daily caps, bounce and complaint monitoring with automatic pause
Thin closed-won history gives a weak profileHighStart from an industry template and let the team's own wins take over as they accumulate
Personal data and consent rulesMediumUse business contact details for a stated purpose, honour opt-outs at once, and follow India's DPDP Act and channel rules
Reps rubber-stamp the review laneMediumTrack approval speed; very fast approvals do not count toward earned trust

9.Roadmap

Now (v1)

Find, write, route

Prove reps book more meetings from signal-led drafts, and that trust can be earned without hurting reply rates.

Next (v2)

Multi-threaded accounts

Reach two or three people on the buying committee with coordinated, different messages, and hand warm replies to Qualify.

Later (v3)

Signals from the whole platform

Learn from which prospects become great customers, not just closed deals, so the profile targets retention as well as wins.

Why I'm close to this

I've spent the past year building AI outbound workflows hands-on: profiles from past wins, signal-led account lists, contact verification, and keeping a sending domain healthy. The lanes come from my work on AI demand forecasting at Xorosoft, where routine reorders went through automatically, larger or uncertain ones waited for a planner, and unusual demand was flagged rather than acted on. Outreach has the same shape: most of it is routine, some matters too much to send unseen, and a little is plainly wrong.
