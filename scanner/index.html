How is this score calculated?
  1. Fail rate per check: the share of rows that fail it (for sparse columns, the share of columns).
  2. Check score = max(0, 1 − 2 × fail rate). 10% failing scores 0.8; once half the rows fail, it scores 0.
  3. Readiness score = 100 × Σ(weight × check score) ÷ Σ(weights), rounded down, over the checks that apply to this file. Weights: Blocker 5, High 3, Medium 2, Low 1. Stale records are shown, not scored.
  4. Blocker cap: if more than 5% of rows fail the Blocker check, the score cannot exceed 64.

The problem

Superleap commits to go-live dates before it has seen the prospect's data. Effort is estimated from conversations, so duplicates, unassigned records, broken dates and inconsistent stage values are discovered during the load. That stretches timelines, adds forward-deployed engineering hours, and risks the deployment guarantee.

Who it's for

  • Superleap AEs, who run a scan on a sample export during evaluation
  • Superleap deployment leads, who run a full scan at kickoff
  • The prospect's CRM admin, who gets the issues report to fix at the source
  • The prospect's sponsor, who sees migration risk before signing

How it works

  • Load a CSV export; everything runs in the browser and nothing is uploaded
  • 12 rule-based checks: required fields, duplicates, emails, dates, owner mapping, references, phones, picklist drift, encoding, sparse columns, length and stale records
  • A 0 to 100 readiness score with a band and the next step for the AE
  • Three exports: issues report, owner list for mapping, de-duplicated CSV

What we'd measure

  • North star: share of deals reaching proposal stage with a readiness scan
  • Go-live accuracy for scanned vs unscanned deals
  • Rows rejected on first load, and cleanup hours per migration
  • Guardrails: data stays on the device, and clean files do not score below 85

First version

One CSV at a time, 12 checks, the score, drill-down and three exports. Out of scope for v1: loading data into Superleap, API connections to other CRMs, AI field mapping, logins and saved history, and cross-file relationship checks.

Risks and open questions

  • Prospects may decline to share data before signing; browser-only processing and a small sample export help
  • Superleap's own required fields are not public, so the checks follow common CRM import rules
  • AEs may skip the scan under deal pressure, so it has to take under five minutes

Research behind it

Data quality is consistently named as a leading cause of CRM projects missing their goals. Retail Insider cites Gartner on poor data quality as the leading cause of CRM failure, and Panorama Consulting on data migration as the biggest implementation challenge for 62% of organisations. LowCode Agency puts reported CRM failure rates between 30 and 55 percent. The full evidence is in the PRD.

Why I'm close to this

At Xorosoft I've moved customers off Shopify and legacy systems many times, carrying over customer records, contacts, price lists, credit limits and order history. The same patterns kept appearing in customers' source data: duplicates, unmapped values and missing references caused most import issues, and the fix belonged in that source data. Every check in this scanner comes from that experience.
